# AI Use Reflection

I used OpenAI Codex for three distinct purposes. For research support, it located candidate primary sources from the Australian Government, Jobs and Skills Australia, the ABS, the Productivity Commission and the ILO. I checked the original publications rather than treating AI output as evidence. For argument-building, it helped organise the impacts, compare policy options and draft indicative costs. For writing and technical support, it edited prose, formatted Markdown and assisted with Git setup.


One correction concerned Jobs and Skills Australia’s figures that 13% of tasks may be automated and about 55% augmented. I rejected an early formulation that treated these as predicted outcomes. After checking the report, I described them as assumptions in an illustrative scenario, not a forecast. I also labelled program costs as this proposal’s estimates because they are not an existing government commitment.

The main limitation was that AI can produce confident wording before the evidence justifies it. It can also blur exposure to a technology with actual adoption or job loss, and generate plausible-looking citations or precise costings without a valid basis. I reduced these risks by preferring official sources, checking publication dates and links, distinguishing evidence from assumptions, and retaining explicit uncertainty and evaluation mechanisms in the recommendation.
